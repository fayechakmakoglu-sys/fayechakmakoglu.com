My Journey

My Journey

How curiosity, opportunity, and a willingness to learn shaped my career in database engineering.

The Beginning

An Unexpected Opportunity

I began at MD Management as a Mac Systems Administrator, providing local IT support for the Cambridge office. About a year later, I learned that my position was being eliminated.

Rather than lose me, the team at Practice Solutions Software found another opportunity for me within the organization. Although I had never worked with Oracle, they believed I could learn quickly and offered me a junior Database Analyst role.

That opportunity changed the course of my career.

Building the Foundation

Learning Oracle from the Ground Up

Starting with no Oracle experience, I learned how the database interacted with the application, how to diagnose issues, and how to recognize the quirks that only appear in real production environments.

I built database servers for healthcare clinics, installed and configured Oracle on Windows, created schemas and tablespaces, configured Transparent Data Encryption, and prepared systems for deployment.

My responsibilities expanded to include RMAN and Data Pump backup scripts, database restores, new-clinic migrations, and work on the first-generation hosted ASP platform.

Real-World Recovery

Learning to Solve Problems Under Pressure

Severe weather, power failures, hardware problems, and failed backups sometimes left clinic databases corrupted or unavailable. Restoring service required careful troubleshooting, adaptability, and a strong understanding of both Oracle and the Windows systems supporting it.

Those early recoveries taught me to remain calm, work methodically, and focus on restoring reliable service with as little data loss as possible.

I learned early that database administration was not simply about maintaining Oracle. It was about keeping healthcare professionals working and protecting the information they relied on every day.
