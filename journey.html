My Journey

My Journey

How curiosity, opportunity, and a willingness to learn shaped my career in database engineering.

The Beginning

An Unexpected Opportunity

I began at MD Management as a Mac Systems Administrator, providing local IT support for the Cambridge office. About a year later, I learned that my position was being eliminated.

Rather than lose me, the team at Practice Solutions Software found another opportunity for me within the organization. Although I had never worked with Oracle, they believed I could learn quickly and offered me a junior Database Analyst role.

That opportunity changed the course of my career.

Building the Foundation

Learning Oracle from the Ground Up

Starting with no Oracle experience, I learned how the database interacted with the application, how to diagnose issues, and how to recognize the quirks that only appear in real production environments.

I built database servers for healthcare clinics, installed and configured Oracle on Windows, created schemas and tablespaces, configured Transparent Data Encryption, and prepared systems for deployment.

My responsibilities expanded to include RMAN and Data Pump backup scripts, database restores, new-clinic migrations, and work on the first-generation hosted ASP platform.

Real-World Recovery

Learning to Solve Problems Under Pressure

Severe weather, power failures, hardware problems, and failed backups sometimes left clinic databases corrupted or unavailable. Restoring service required careful troubleshooting, adaptability, and a strong understanding of both Oracle and the Windows systems supporting it.

Those early recoveries taught me to remain calm, work methodically, and focus on restoring reliable service with as little data loss as possible.

I learned early that database administration was not simply about maintaining Oracle. It was about keeping healthcare professionals working and protecting the information they relied on every day.

BlackBerry

Learning at Scale

Joining BlackBerry meant starting over once again. Although I already had Oracle experience, I had never worked with Linux or large-scale enterprise infrastructure. Suddenly I was immersed in Red Hat Enterprise Linux, Oracle Grid Infrastructure, ASM, Data Guard Broker, Fast-Start Failover, observers, cascaded standbys, and globally distributed production services.

The learning curve was steep. It took nearly six months before I felt comfortable joining the on-call rotation. There was a saying on the team that one year at BlackBerry was equal to five years somewhere else. Looking back, they were right. Every day felt like drinking from a fire hose.

As my confidence grew, so did my responsibilities. I built production databases supporting BlackBerry Messenger, participated in opening a new data centre, migrated regional services from the United States into Canada with zero downtime, and learned how BBM worked from user registration all the way through the backend infrastructure serving customers around the world.

Beyond Oracle, I learned to think about entire systems rather than individual databases. I balanced BBM registration loads, supported large-scale migrations, contributed to LOB storage initiatives, and eventually worked across Oracle, SQL Server, MySQL, EnterpriseDB PostgreSQL, and Cassandra environments.

During my time there I also introduced the team's first Confluence knowledge base, documenting build procedures, methods of procedure, common fixes, and operational standards to improve consistency across the DBA team.

In less than four years I grew from a junior DBA into one of the team's most trusted database administrators. More than anything, BlackBerry taught me how to learn quickly, remain calm under pressure, and solve problems in some of the most demanding production environments imaginable.
